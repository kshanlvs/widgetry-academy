A step-by-step plan for students and freshers to get a Flutter internship or first job in India: skills, projects, resume, where to apply and interviews.

Getting your first Flutter job is the hardest one. Every listing seems to want "1 to 2 years of experience", and when you apply to internships you hear nothing back. The good news: Flutter teams hire freshers all the time. They just hire the freshers who make it easy to say yes.

This guide is the plan I give students who ask me how to get their first Flutter internship or fresher job in India. It doesn't need a famous college or a referral. It needs about two to three months of focused work.

## What companies actually expect from a Flutter fresher

Nobody expects a fresher to know everything. When I look at a fresher's application, I'm checking four things:

- **Can you build a screen from a design?** Layouts with `Row`, `Column`, `Stack`, lists, forms and navigation.
- **Can you talk to a server?** Calling a REST API, parsing JSON into Dart models, and showing loading and error states.
- **Do you manage state sensibly?** One approach, used properly: Provider, Riverpod or Bloc. You don't need all three.
- **Can you finish something?** One complete, working app is worth more than five half-built tutorials.

Add **Git and GitHub** to that list. If your code isn't on GitHub, the reviewer has nothing to look at.

## Step 1: Learn the core, not everything

Freshers lose months trying to learn every package. Focus on this order:

1. **Dart basics**: types, null safety, classes, `async`/`await` and `Future`.
2. **Widgets and layout**: stateless vs stateful widgets, building responsive screens.
3. **Navigation**: moving between screens and passing data (go_router is common in company code).
4. **APIs and JSON**: the `http` or `dio` package, model classes, error handling.
5. **State management**: pick one and build a full app with it.
6. **Local storage and Firebase basics**: shared_preferences, Firebase Auth and Firestore.

If you want a full roadmap with timelines, read [How to become a Flutter developer](how-to-become-flutter-developer.html).

## Step 2: Build two projects that look like real work

Recruiters see the same to-do and calculator apps every day. Build things that look like the apps companies make:

- **A shopping app**: product list from an API, product page, cart, and checkout screen. This covers lists, navigation, state and APIs in one project.
- **An app with login and data**: Firebase Auth plus Firestore, for example a simple expense tracker or attendance app.

For each project, do three things most freshers skip:

- Write a short **README** with screenshots and what you built.
- Handle **loading, empty and error states**. This is the clearest sign you think like a developer.
- If you can, **publish it**: an APK on GitHub releases, or the Play Store. A link someone can try beats a video.

## Step 3: Make a resume that gets past the filter

Many companies use software that scans resumes before a human sees them. Keep yours to one page, in a simple single-column format, and put the words from the job post in it (Flutter, Dart, Firebase, REST API, Bloc and so on) where they're true.

Write project lines as results, not tasks. "Built a shopping app in Flutter with Bloc, REST APIs and a working cart" says much more than "Worked on a Flutter project".

Our [Flutter developer resume guide](flutter-developer-resume.html) has example bullet points, and the [free resume builder](../resume-builder.html) makes an ATS-friendly PDF in a few minutes.

## Step 4: Where to find Flutter internships and fresher jobs

Spread your search across a few places instead of applying to hundreds of listings on one site:

- **Niche job boards.** [Widgetry Jobs](../jobs.html) lists only Flutter, Android and iOS roles, and every post is checked by a working mobile engineer. Choose **Fresher** under experience or **Internship** under job type to see only those.
- **Startup careers pages.** Small startups often hire interns directly. Search Google for `"Flutter intern"` together with your city.
- **LinkedIn.** Use the "Past week" filter and the "Internship" or "Entry level" experience filter. Apply early; older posts are often already full.
- **Internship platforms** such as Internshala, for short or remote internships while you study.
- **Your own network.** Seniors from your college, Flutter community meetups and Discord groups. A short message with your GitHub link works better than a long one.

You can also make a free [Talent Network profile](../talent.html), so companies hiring mobile developers can find you instead of the other way round.

## Step 5: Apply the smart way

- **Apply to fewer jobs, better.** Ten tailored applications beat a hundred copy-paste ones.
- **Mention one thing about the company.** "I used your app and noticed..." shows you care.
- **Put your best project link in the first two lines** of your message or cover note.
- **Follow up once** after about a week. Politely, and only once.

## Step 6: Prepare for the interview

Fresher Flutter interviews usually have three parts:

1. **Basics**: stateless vs stateful widgets, `setState`, keys, `Future` vs `Stream`, how `build` works.
2. **Your project**: expect "Why did you use this package?" and "What would you change?" Know every line of the projects on your resume.
3. **A small task**: build a screen, fix a bug, or call an API, sometimes live and sometimes as a take-home.

Go through our [Flutter interview questions and answers](flutter-interview-questions.html). Then do at least one practice interview with a working developer, out loud, with a timer. Answering in your head and answering to a person feel very different.

## Watch out for internship scams

Some "internships" are really paid courses in disguise. Be careful if:

- They ask you to **pay a fee** for training, registration, a laptop or a certificate.
- They promise a job "after you complete our paid program".
- The offer comes on WhatsApp with no interview and no company email.

A genuine employer never asks you to pay to get hired. If you see a job like this on Widgetry Jobs, use **Report this job** on the job page and we'll remove it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a Flutter job without a CS degree?

Yes. Flutter teams care more about what you can build than your degree. A clear GitHub profile with two finished apps is often enough for internships and many fresher roles. Some larger companies do filter by degree, so apply widely.

### How long does it take to become job-ready in Flutter?

With two to three hours of practice a day, most beginners can build a complete app in about two to three months. Getting your first offer can take another one to three months of applying, depending on your city and how actively you apply.

### Are remote Flutter internships available?

Yes, especially at startups. On Widgetry Jobs, choose "Remote" under work mode. Remote internships are competitive, so a published app helps you stand out.

### Is an unpaid internship worth it?

A short unpaid internship can be worth it if you work on a real app with a real team and get a proper certificate and reference. Never pay to do an internship.

### Should I learn native Android or iOS first?

No. You can start with Flutter directly. Learning a little about how Android and iOS builds work (Gradle, signing, permissions) will help later, but it isn't needed for your first role.
